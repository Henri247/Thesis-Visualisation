CHAPTER 01 — THEORY

Value and environmental costs can travel in different directions.

The dissertation combines Marxist value theory, unequal exchange and ecological unequal exchange to interpret different positions in the EV chain.

UNEQUAL EXCHANGE

Where value is produced ≠ where it is captured

Wage differentials, capital-intensity differences and market power are used to interpret cross-border value transfers.

ECOLOGICAL UNEQUAL EXCHANGE

Environmental costs are geographically asymmetric

The framework asks whether ecological benefits in core economies depend on resource extraction and pollution being concentrated elsewhere.

JUST TRANSITION

Who bears decarbonisation's social costs?

The final question is whether a cleaner European transport system can be called just if upstream regions absorb disproportionate social and ecological harm.

CONCEPTUAL LINK

The thesis connects undervalued labour and exports to undervalued environmental space: economic unequal exchange becomes part of the mechanism producing ecological unequal exchange.
